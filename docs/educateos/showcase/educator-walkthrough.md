# Educator walkthrough, by grade band

- Issue: SCHO-327. Rehearsed on <https://educateos.io/demo/>, 2026-11-02.

For teachers, curriculum leads and principals. Each band below is self-contained: open
`https://educateos.io/demo/`, choose the band's student or teacher in the picker, and follow it.
Personas share one district, so what the teacher does, the student and family see. **Reset demo**
starts everyone over.

Every band uses the same parts:

- **Authoring.** A teacher builds an activity under **Create activity** from a template (writing
  task, read-and-respond, picture response, problem set, primary-source analysis, science
  investigation), attaches readings from **Resources**, signs a rubric, sets a due date, and
  chooses how much AI help is allowed (none, hints only or full; every showcase activity uses none
  or hints only). Activities sit in **Modules** beside short lessons.
- **Student work.** Saved as revisions (**History**), handed in as attempts with a receipt code.
- **Feedback and grades.** The teacher scores each rubric criterion, adds comments, then
  **Approve official grade** and **Share feedback with student** as separate decisions. Only
  approved grades reach the gradebook; only shared feedback reaches the student and family.
- **Tutor.** Each band has its own tutor. On the demo it is a labelled, scripted simulation. Under
  each message it shows what Hall Monitor, which screens every tutor message on the live district,
  would decide: allowed, redirected (off topic, or asking the tutor to do graded work), or held
  (personal details, neither sent nor kept). Student AI on the live district is a pilot preview on
  synthetic identities only; see the [showcase README](README.md#maturity-caveats-what-not-to-claim).

## K-2: Mateo (grade 1) and Mr. Yazzie

**What the teacher authors.** Grade 1 Reading Foundations has two modules, "Short Vowel Sounds" and
"Stories We Love", with picture-rich mini-lessons ("The Little Seed" is told in four short lines).
"Retell The Little Seed" is a read-and-respond activity: a Listen step that reads the story, two
spoken answers ("What happened first?", "What happened next?") and a drawn answer ("Draw what
happened last"). Its rubric uses young-learner levels: Not yet, Getting there, Got it!

**What the student does.** Mateo opens Retell The Little Seed from Grade 1 Reading Foundations. The
young-learner view shows one step a screen with big type: **Let's start**, then each step's **Read
to me** button reads it aloud. Sentence starters ("First, ___.", "Next, ___.", "Last, ___.") can each
be read aloud. He taps **Record**, speaks, **Stop**, listens back, **Save my recording**. On the
drawing step he picks a crayon and draws with a finger, pen or mouse, then **Save my drawing**, or
adds a photo. **Finish** shows what is saved, and **I'm done!** hands it in.

**What feedback and grades look like.** Open Word Builders and go to its last screen: "What your
teacher said" shows the official grade (6 out of 6), Mr. Yazzie's comment ("You are a
word-building star, Mateo!") and each criterion's score. **Grades** in the header lists every
official grade.

**As Mr. Yazzie.** Grade 1 Reading Foundations, **Submissions**, Retell The Little Seed, **Review
Priya Natarajan's work**: her two recordings play in the page and her drawing shows full size. Score
both criteria, add a comment, approve the grade, share the feedback. Then Grade 1 Mathematics,
Submissions, Apple Orchard Story Problems, **Review Jonah Fischer's work**: it is scored with a
comment but not yet shared, so **Share feedback with student** sends it to Jonah and his family.
**Gradebook** shows both classes' official grades. (The seeded classmates' recordings are short
hummed tunes standing in for voices. To hear a real retelling, record one as Mateo first, hand it
in, and open his.)

**What Reading Buddy does and won't do.** Reads the story again, asks one small question at a time
("Who helped the seed?"), gives sound-by-sound hints ("It starts with r-r-r"). It keeps to the
story: "Tell me a scary story" is redirected ("Let's keep our story cozy today!"), and contact
details get its privacy reply. It reminds the child it is a computer helper, not a person.

## 3-5: Abena (grade 4) and Ms. Al-Sayed

**What the teacher authors.** Grade 4 ELA has "Finding the Theme" and "Opinion Writing" modules.
"Should Recess Be Longer?" is a writing task in three steps, Plan, Draft and Revise, with a rubric
and AI help set to hints only. Ms. Al-Sayed also keeps a **Note to families** under Announcements,
included in the weekly family email.

**What the student does.** Abena opens Should Recess Be Longer? Instructions and each step have
**Read to me**. Her draft is already started; she continues it in the Draft box, **Save**, and
**Submit** when the three steps are done.

**What feedback and grades look like.** On "The Theme of 'The Kite That Would Not Fly'", scroll to
**Grade and feedback**: official grade 11 out of 12, the comment ("your theme statement is clear and
your two details prove it. Watch for run-on sentences"), and Theme, Evidence and Conventions scores
out of 4.

**As Ms. Al-Sayed.** Submissions, The Theme of "The Kite That Would Not Fly", **Review Hana
Suzuki's work**. Pick a level for each criterion (Beginning, Developing, Proficient, Advanced) and
**Save score**; the official grade fills in from the scores (for example 9 out of 12).
**Approve official grade** asks once more, naming the student and the grade. Then **Gradebook**:
Hana's grade is there beside Abena's and Diego's. Post an announcement from **Announcements**.

**What the Homework Helper does and won't do.** Breaks the task into steps ("Step 1: decide your
opinion"), offers sentence starters ("One reason is ___. For example, ___."), and offers to check
two things in her reason so she can fix it herself. "Just write it for me" is redirected ("I can't
write it for you, but I know you can do it!"). An address or phone number gets its privacy reply.
Typing anything outside the script gets "This preview follows a script".

## 6-8: Chidi (grade 7) and Mr. Ortega

**What the teacher authors.** World History 7 has "Ancient Mesopotamia" and "The Silk Roads"
modules. "What Do Hammurabi's Laws Tell Us?" asks for sourcing, context, and a claim with evidence
from at least two laws, against a four-level rubric. A class discussion, "Which ancient invention
matters most today?", sits in the Silk Roads module.

**What the student does.** Chidi reads his released feedback on Hammurabi's laws, replies in the
discussion from the class page (open the discussion, write in **Your post**, **Post**, or **Reply**
to a classmate), and drafts "Ideas on the Move".

**What feedback and grades look like.** Grade and feedback at the bottom of the Hammurabi activity:
11 out of 12, the comment ("Excellent comparison of laws 196 and 198, Chidi..."), and Sourcing,
Context and Claim scores.

**As Mr. Ortega.** Submissions, What Do Hammurabi's Laws Tell Us?, **Review Felix Brandt's work**,
score and approve. **Discussions** to reply to the class. **Gradebook** shows official grades
only, with excused, missing and late marks set from a cell, and **Export CSV**.

**What the Study Coach does and won't do.** Asks for the evidence behind a claim ("Which law shows
that? Quote it, then explain"), explains why sourcing matters, and gives draft feedback as a
strength and next steps. "Can you write my paragraph?" is redirected to planning it together.

## 9-12: Amara (grade 9) and Mr. Rivera

**What the teacher authors.** English 9 has "The Power of Argument" and "Coming-of-Age Stories"
units. "Should Community Service Be Required to Graduate?" is an argument essay requiring a precise
claim, evidence from at least one source, reasoning and a fair counterclaim, scored on Claim,
Evidence, Counterclaim and Conventions. "What Changes a Character?" is the next essay, and the unit
has a discussion.

**What the student does.** Amara reads her released feedback and official grade on the community
service essay, then plans "What Changes a Character?" with the coach and saves her draft.

**What feedback and grades look like.** 15 out of 16, the comment ("A nuanced claim and a graceful
rebuttal, Amara. Quote the brief directly..."), and a score per criterion. The course grade and
letter are under **Grades**.

**As Mr. Rivera.** Submissions, Should Community Service Be Required to Graduate?, **Review Elena
Sato's work**: score, comment, approve (12 out of 16 from the rubric), share. **Gradebook**,
**Announcements**.

**What the Research & Essay Coach does and won't do.** Tests whether a thesis is arguable, checks
whether evidence supports the claim, names the strongest counterargument, and checks citations
against what she tells it (it says it cannot open links). "Write my conclusion for me" is
redirected to a sentence shape she fills in herself. "Find me a statistic to cite" is refused: it
will not invent sources or numbers, and sends her to the library databases.

## Families and the district

**Rosa Delgado** has Mateo (grade 1) and Lucía (grade 10). Each child's page lists their classes
with "Graded so far". A class shows announcements, due dates, official grades and the comments a
teacher has shared, and "No feedback yet" until then. She never sees a child's drafts or personal
space.

**Avery Admin** sees every class, its school, term, teachers and student count under **All
classes**, people and records under **Manage classes and people**, **Learning standards** to
approve (frameworks whose licences allow it ship built in; NGSS does not yet), **Policies**, **AI
budgets** and **Term rollover**.

## Questions educators ask

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Can I turn the tutor off for my class? | Each activity sets AI help: none, hints only, or full. The district sets budgets and policies, and a class can be stricter than the district, never less strict. |
| Does the AI grade? | No. Automated checks and suggested scores, where a rubric has them, count only once the teacher saves them as their own and approves the grade. |
| Do families see drafts? | No. Families see what the teacher has shared, after it is shared. |
| Where do grades go? | The EducateOS gradebook, and where the district's grade of record is another system (Canvas, Google Classroom), teacher-approved grades are passed back to it. |
| Which standards are in it? | Those whose publishers allow their text to be shared, such as CCSS. NGSS is pointer-only and not built in. |
