# Student-led learning pilot: evaluation plan

- Status: **Plan, not results.** No child has used this. Nothing here has been run.
- Issue: SCHO-432. Pairs with [learning-pilot-guide.md](learning-pilot-guide.md).

## The rule behind this plan

Green tests and a clean replay show the software follows its rules. They are **not evidence that
children learn more**. Two things can pass every check and still fail a child: a lesson that
teaches badly, and a teacher's "unseen task" that is easier or harder than it looks. Claims about
learning come only from an independent measure that the planner never touches.

## Questions

1. **Learning.** Do students who use the planner on unit rates (6.RP.A.3b, 7.RP.A.1) and argument
   writing (W.6.1) show greater gains on an independent measure than comparable students who do not?
2. **Retention and transfer.** Does the gain last a month, and does it show on an unfamiliar problem?
3. **Trust.** Do teachers' "independent-reviewed" judgments agree with the independent measure?
4. **Use and safety.** Do students choose it, disclose help honestly, skip freely, and avoid harm?

## Design

- **Independent pre, post and delayed test.** Use the district's existing benchmark items, released
  state-test items or a short form a district math or ELA lead selects. It must not be written,
  generated or tuned by the planner, its lessons or any model, and must not share items with the
  practice challenges or the teacher's unseen tasks.
  - **Pre:** the week before first use.
  - **Post:** within one week after the last session.
  - **Delayed:** four to six weeks after the post, with no further planner use in between. (The
    planner's own seven-day delayed check is a teacher observation, not this measure.)
  - **Transfer items:** at least a third of items in an unfamiliar context (not games, cookies or sports).
  - Items cover the standards used. For 6.RP.A.3b use items for unit rates, unit pricing and constant
    speed only, not the whole ratio domain.
- **Comparison.** Best: classes or students assigned by lottery to start now or later (waitlist).
  Otherwise, matched classes in the same school with the same teacher where possible. Without a
  comparison, report gains as descriptive only, not as an effect.
- **Blinded scoring.** Scorers do not know group or time point. Use a written rubric; double-score
  a sample and report agreement.
- **Pre-register.** Write the primary outcome, analysis and stop rules before the pre-test.
- **Sample.** A founding pilot (a few teachers, one school) will be too small to detect modest
  effects. Report counts and intervals; do not claim significance from a handful of classes.

## Measures

| Measure | Source | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Primary:** post and delayed score on the independent test, adjusted for pre | Independent test | The learning claim |
| Transfer-item score | Independent test | Transfer, not memorised context |
| Agreement: teacher "independent-reviewed" vs. independent score | Replay export plus test | Is the evidence floor trustworthy? |
| Offer rate, accept rate, skip rate, share rate | Planner/coverage counts | Real choice, not compliance |
| Help disclosure mix (independent, adult, peer, AI, unknown) | Planner counts | Honest disclosure; high "unknown" is a warning |
| Share of goals with no natural alignment | Planner | Is the matcher too narrow? |
| Teacher minutes per week | Teacher check-in (self-reported) | Cost of review |
| Student and family experience | Short survey; no scores of personality or emotion | Understandable, voluntary, fair |
| Incidents and complaints | Issue log | Safety |

CASEL-style classroom skills are optional practice. They are **not outcomes**, are never scored, and
are not used to profile any student.

## Fairness and safety checks

- Compare offer, skip and independent-review rates across groups the district already reports
  (disability and language services, grade, class) in aggregate, only where group sizes are large
  enough not to identify anyone. Investigate gaps; do not "fix" with hidden nudges.
- Confirm families who opted out lost nothing, and that no grade changed because of planner activity.
- Review a sample of tasks offered for weak interest-to-standard fits (the matcher is keyword-based).
- Review any AI-assisted help separately; AI is off by default and never in K-5.

## Stop rules

Stop or pause and review if: a student is shown a standard the registry does not hold; any private
goal appears to a teacher or family; family suggestions are shown without an opt-in; a guardian sees work
or a grade; students report distress or feeling profiled; or the independent test shows lower
post scores than comparison with an interval that excludes no difference.

## What each result lets you say

| Result | Allowed statement | Not allowed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Replay and tests green | "The software enforces its evidence rules on synthetic data." | "It improves learning." |
| Pre/post gain, no comparison | "Scores rose in this group." | "The planner caused it." |
| Gain beats comparison, large enough sample, scorers blinded | "Associated with a gain beyond comparison, with these limits." | Generalising to other districts or grades |
| Delayed gain holds on transfer items | "Retention and transfer were observed in this pilot." | "Mastery" of a standard or domain |

## Before running with real children

District DPA signed; family letter sent; retention decided and written down; teachers trained on
the unseen-task rule; independent test selected and checked for overlap; scoring plan and
pre-registration done; a browser, assistive-technology and device pass done (not covered here).
