Overview

A parent should be able to answer three questions: What should we practice? What can my child do independently? What should we try next? A child should be able to start a short activity, hear the directions, respond without typing, and finish with a clear stopping point.

Build a family workspace with a small, reviewed content library, an adult-controlled practice queue, and progress grounded in actual work. Grade is a starting suggestion; reading and math can begin at different skill levels. The adult can adjust either sequence without erasing evidence.

This is a product proposal, not a claim that family onboarding, structured phonics practice, interactive number lines, or family progress reports already work. Activity length is a design default, not a required daily dose or an efficacy claim.

Approaches considered

Guided family practice

Recommended. Reviewed skill sequences, a ready-to-use daily queue, parent content controls, and evidence for each next step.

Requires family permissions and structured activity records; gives the clearest connection between practice and progress.

Parent resource organizer

Save worksheets, books, and links, then manually record what happened.

Smaller first build, but much more teaching and progress interpretation falls on the adult.

Open tutor conversations

Extend the existing Reading Buddy into a general home tutor.

Reuses the conversational experience, but conversation history alone cannot establish skill growth or a systematic instructional sequence.

Goals & Non-goals

Goals

  • Parents choose, preview, order, pause, repeat, and replace practice.
  • Children practice through listening, tapping, moving objects, and optional speaking.
  • Reading follows an explicit, cumulative sound–spelling sequence.
  • Math connects quantities, number lines, and equations.
  • Progress explains the evidence, assistance used, and a useful next activity.

Outside the first release

  • School assignments, roster sync, teacher dashboards, and official grades.
  • A complete K–2 curriculum or diagnostic reading assessment.
  • Unrestricted child chat and automatic AI-generated lessons.
  • Automatic pronunciation grading or claims about dyslexia.
  • Social feeds, leaderboards, and rewards for time spent.

The daily learning loop

  1. Parent choosesAccept a suggested reading or math activity, preview it, and adjust the queue.
  2. Child practicesOne spoken instruction and one task at a time, with repeat and help controls.
  3. Work is savedSave responses and assistance used. Resume an interrupted session without losing completed steps.
  4. Parent reviewsSee what happened, inspect examples, and choose whether to repeat or continue.

Reading: an explicit path from sounds to meaning

Use a reviewed scope and sequence. Teach a small step explicitly, model it, practice together, then try a fresh example independently. Revisit earlier learning rather than relying on a single correct response.

For unfamiliar written words, prompt attention to letters, sounds, and blending. Pictures can support meaning after decoding; they should not become a word-guessing strategy. Use reviewed sound recordings for phoneme models: ordinary browser speech is useful for directions, but is not a reliable source of isolated letter sounds.

First content pack: a small set of reviewed sound–letter correspondences, blending and segmenting, CVC word building, and matching decodable sentences. Add digraphs, final-e patterns, vowel teams, and more advanced word work in later reviewed packs. Keep read-aloud vocabulary and comprehension in the initial experience.

Math: make numbers visible

Begin with counting, matching quantities to numerals, comparing, and composing numbers. Introduce a number line as a model of distance and change. Start within 10, then extend to 20 as the learner is ready; tens, ones, and larger ranges follow.

First content pack: count and compare within 10; place a number; find one more or one less; and represent addition and subtraction with counters and number-line jumps. Extend within 20 after review. Record the starting point, jumps, answer, and help used so a parent can understand errors such as counting the starting mark as the first jump.

Two views, built around different jobs

Parent · Today / Library / Progress

Manage the learning

Add a child profile, choose a starting skill for each track, and set a flexible routine. Preview and save reviewed packs. Reorder, pause, repeat, or replace activities per child.

Keep books, worksheets, and links on a family shelf with optional notes. Separate a saved resource from a structured activity that can produce skill evidence. Source import and activity adaptation require adult review before a child sees them.

Review recent work, assistance used, and the recommended next step. Switch between children without combining their histories.

Child · My practice

A clear next action

Open the activity the parent selected. Hear directions, see a worked example, try a task, and finish. Use large targets, calm feedback, replay, and an obvious “ask my grown-up” action.

Support touch, keyboard, and a tap alternative to dragging. Respect reduced motion and provide text alongside audio. Microphone access is optional; a parent can observe oral work without recording it.

Changing settings or viewing adult controls requires an authenticated adult context enforced by the server.

Progress that explains itself

Use parent-facing states such as Not tried, Practicing with help, Practicing independently, and Ready to revisit. Show how many fresh examples were attempted, when, what support was given, and who observed the result. A finished activity is not proof of mastery.

Illustrative parent note: “Today your child blended 4 of 5 new short-a words without a prompt. One word needed you to model the first sound. Next time, try another short-a set before adding a new vowel.”

Each observation needs a child, skill, content and item version, timestamp, response or adult observation, support used, and source. Derive summaries from those observations. Keep modeled practice separate from independent checks, and mark unobserved speech as “not assessed.” Let adults correct an observation with history retained. A parent choosing to advance changes the queue, not the recorded evidence.

Any future “secure” or “mastered” label needs a published, educator-reviewed rule using repeated independent performance across sessions. Speech transcription alone is insufficient evidence of accurate decoding. Time spent, streaks, and chat length are not reading or math outcomes.

Architecture and existing foundations

Introduce an explicit family ownership boundary with child-scoped learning access. Reuse current content versioning, activity, artifact, and assessment mechanisms where they fit. Determine the minimum new family contracts before adding a parallel learning system.

Important boundary: school guardians are intentionally read-only under ADR-0012. A parent managing home learning needs separate family authority. Family membership must not grant school record access or mutate school guardian permissions.

Data flow and failure behavior

An authenticated parent selects a pinned activity version for a child. The server checks family and child authority when starting and saving practice. Save completed responses incrementally with idempotent writes; resuming cannot duplicate an attempt. Progress reads only that child's authorized observations. An adult-only edit changes future practice without rewriting past work.

On save failure, keep the current response available for retry and clearly show that it has not saved. On denied microphone access, offer the adult-observed path. If optional AI is unavailable or screening fails, stop that AI interaction; reviewed deterministic practice can continue through a separate path. Do not silently substitute a different lesson or scoring method.

Model fields above are proposed requirements, not new schema declarations. Final contracts must follow existing vocabulary, retention, authorization, audit, and versioning rules.

Risks & Mitigations

RiskMitigation
“Science of reading” becomes an unsupported label.Publish the actual scope and sequence; obtain qualified literacy review; distinguish instructional design from demonstrated efficacy.
Progress overstates independence or transfers between unlike tasks.Record help and evidence source. Keep listening, decoding, and spelling separate; use fresh independent checks across sessions.
A child inherits an adult's permissions on a shared device.Use server-scoped child access and adult reauthentication for management. A hidden button or local PIN alone is insufficient.
Speech technology misjudges a young or multilingual speaker.No automatic oral-reading grade in v1. Make observation and recording optional, and expose uncertainty.
Family operation conflicts with existing deployment assumptions.Resolve tenancy and the operator's role in phase 1. Do not claim that a school guardian route or the current control plane already hosts families.
More child data is retained than practice needs.Default to structured responses and minimal profile details; avoid a full birth date. Specify retention, deletion, and a scoped family export before launch; recordings require a deliberate adult choice.

Verification

  • First complete slice: a parent adds two children without a school, chooses a reading activity for one and a math activity for the other, previews both, launches child practice, resumes after an interruption, and reviews evidence and a next step. Each child sees only their practice.
  • Authorization: API and harness checks cover another family, another child, revoked adult access, expired child access, and attempts to invoke parent-only actions. Existing school guardian restrictions remain unchanged.
  • Instruction: qualified review of the sound–spelling sequence and recordings; item checks verify decodability and correct number-line starts, directions, jump counts, and endpoints. Adult-read prompts cannot count as independent decoding evidence.
  • Experience: frontend browser tests cover touch alternatives, keyboard access, focus, replay, no microphone, screen-reader labels, and visible save failure. A live browser checks final wiring where needed.
  • Repository checks when implemented: run relevant package test:run suites, pnpm curriculum:check for content changes, and the required workspace lint/build checks before pushing. Use API/harness tests for persisted state and refusals; model request bodies need unit or integration tests.
  • Pilot outcomes: observe setup completion, ability to start the intended activity, return to practice, the parent's understanding of evidence, and performance on fresh independent items. Operational events contain no child responses or recordings. No learning-gain claim follows from engagement alone.

Open Questions

  • Resolved: parent-managed home learning is the first audience; no school-assigned content dependency.
  • Default proposal: start with reviewed English-language foundational packs. Reading and math placement are independent, and a short parent-guided starting check is optional.
  • Resolved (ADR-0016): each community gets a dedicated installation, operated by its organizers like a district. Families sign in through the community's Access policy and join with open, approval or invite-only sign-up. A child is a learner profile, never a principal, and practices inside a server-enforced child session.
  • Before content release: choose the first sound–spelling set and content reviewer; define evidence rules and the initial supported skill range. Market K–2 as the intended audience, not as a completed three-year curriculum.
  • Before recordings or multiple caregivers ship: settle consent, retention, account recovery, invitations, revocation, and the visibility each caregiver receives.
