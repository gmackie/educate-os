Founding pilot · For facilitators

Teacher kickoff training: 60 minutes

For whoever runs the founding pilot's kickoff with the school's champion teachers: an EducateOS team member, the school's pilot lead, or both. Everything hands-on happens in the public demo, so no one touches real student data.

Before the session

  • The demo works on the school's devices and network. Open educateos.io/demo on a teacher laptop on the school network. It is a large page (over 3 MB of script) that runs entirely in the browser, in a current Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox. If a filter blocks it, ask IT to allow educateos.io.
  • Everyone can sign in to the pilot district. Ask the district administrator to add each champion teacher and assign their classes before the day. Anyone who sees "You're signed in, but you haven't been added to this school yet" sends their sign-in ID to the administrator.
  • Know whether AI is on. Ask the district whether its AI workspace is turned on, which models it allows and its monthly cap per person. If it is on, sign in to /ai as yourself and check that a short request works. If it is off, the AI segment is a walk-through only.
  • Name the pilot lead and how teachers reach them. Write it on the quickstart's last page.
  • Print the teacher quickstart, one per teacher, and this outline's checklist.
  • Pick personas. One demo teacher per participant, closest to what they teach.

0:00Welcome (5 min)

Say

  • The pilot is one school, a few teachers, about ten weeks. We want your honest reaction, including what's confusing or missing.
  • The data rule. Until the district signs the data processing agreement, we use made-up students and staff accounts only. No real student names, work or grades, in Teacher Studio or in an AI prompt.
  • The pilot starts with AI for teachers. Student AI helpers are still in development, and the district can keep them off.
  • Today is all in the public demo, a fictional district that runs in your browser.

0:05Open the demo (5 min)

Everyone opens their persona's link. Point out:

  • The Demo banner: fictional district, everything stays in your browser, AI tutors are simulated.
  • My classes and a class card. Open it and read the tabs: Roster, Modules, Resources, Create activity, Submissions, Gradebook, Announcements, Discussions, Quizzes.
  • Roster: who's enrolled and how much they've turned in. The district's roster decides who is in a class.

On the real district a teacher signs in and lands in Teacher Studio. Its menu adds Today, Classes, Gradebook, Calendar, Inbox and, when AI is on, AI workspace.

0:10Hands-on 1: post an assignment (12 min)

  1. Create activity. Pick a template that fits: Writing task for most; Picture response or Read and respond for K-2.
  2. Fill in Title, Overview for students and the template's fields.
  3. AI help: talk through None, Hints only and Full. The district may allow less.
  4. Rubric: choose "The rubric from …" an earlier activity, or create a simple one.
  5. Preview as student, read it, then Publish and assign with a Due date.
  6. Open Submissions and find the new activity.

Watch for: a box titled "A few things to finish" lists what is missing, usually an empty required field. Leaving the due date empty makes it due at the end of the term.

0:22Hands-on 2: a timed quiz with extended time (12 min)

  1. Quizzes, type a Quiz title, Create quiz.
  2. Time limit in minutes (optional): enter 20.
  3. Add two questions, for example Add true or false and Add multiple choice. Fill in each prompt and correct answer.
  4. Save quiz, then Publish.
  5. Scroll to Extended time. Pick a student, pick 1.5× (it shows "1.5× time (30 minutes)"), Give extended time.

Talk through: only the class's teachers and district administrators see the list; each student sees only their own time; an attempt in progress gets the extra time at once; extended time needs a time limit. Once a student starts, the questions lock and the settings stay editable.

0:34Hands-on 3: grade, then read the gradebook (8 min)

Use the persona's own task, which is also in Things to try:

Then open Gradebook: official grades only, the new quiz and activity as columns, a cell's Excused, Missing and Late marks, Gradebook settings and Export CSV.

0:42Your AI tools (10 min, facilitator demonstrates)

The public demo has no AI workspace. Demonstrate on the pilot district if its AI is on, from your own account and with no student information. Otherwise walk through task 6 of the quickstart.

  1. AI workspace in the menu, opening at /ai on the same address. Its home, Teaching companion: Start a conversation and the starters Plan a lesson, Create practice questions, Adapt an activity and Draft a family update.
  2. Run Plan a lesson for a made-up class. Point out that it asks questions first and drafts for you to review.
  3. Ask AI about this on a student's work page, only where AI is on.
  4. Optionally, in the public demo, a student persona's tutor, for example Amara Okafor with the Research & Essay Coach. It is scripted, and under each message shows what Hall Monitor would decide: allowed, redirected or held. That's the student side, and it shows what screening looks like.

Say

  • The district sets the policy: whether AI is on, which models, the monthly cap. A class can be stricter, never looser.
  • Every request goes through the AI call guard in the district's own Cloudflare account. Where the district requires it, Hall Monitor screens the message, and can screen the reply.
  • If something is flagged you get a short neutral message instead of a reply. It doesn't say why or quote you, and nothing else happens. Rephrase or carry on.
  • The AI doesn't grade, and doesn't send anything to students or families. You decide what to publish.
  • The teacher starter blueprints (Week Ahead, Research Kit, Assignment Companion) are in development. Use them only if the district has added them.

Don't try to trigger a flag on purpose on the live district.

0:52Questions (5 min)

See common questions below.

0:57Checklist and next steps (3 min)

Hand out the checklist. Agree the date of the first check-in and how feedback reaches the pilot lead.

Facilitator notes

  • Keep the pace by doing each step on the projector a beat ahead, then circulating.
  • If someone falls behind, they can skip to the next segment: the demo's classes already have work in every state.
  • A participant who reloads loses their changes. Re-open the persona link and carry on.
  • If a participant's district sign-in fails during the session, take their sign-in ID for the administrator and keep them in the demo.
  • Write down every question you can't answer and every "where is…?" moment, and send them to hello@educateos.io after the session. They are the pilot's best evidence.
  • Don't promise features. If it isn't in the quickstart, it is in development or not built yet: say which.

Common questions

QuestionAnswer
Can I use my real class?Not yet. Until the district signs the DPA, use made-up students only. Your pilot lead will say when that changes.
Who adds my students?The district's roster. You can't add students in Teacher Studio.
Does the AI grade?No. Suggested scores count only when you save them as yours and approve the grade.
Can students use the AI workspace?The pilot starts with AI for teachers. Student AI helpers are in development, and the district decides whether to turn them on. On an activity, AI help (None, Hints only, Full) is your choice for students; the district may allow less.
Who sees my AI conversations?The district's AI call guard and, where the district requires it, Hall Monitor check each message. The district keeps no per-message record of those checks; administrators see AI spend. EducateOS staff can't reach the district unless the district lets them.
What if the AI says my message can't be sent?Screening flagged it. Nothing else happens. Rephrase, or tell your pilot lead if it shouldn't have been stopped.
Can I turn AI off for my class?Set AI help to None on each activity. Whether AI is on at all is the district's decision.
Where do grades go?The EducateOS gradebook, official grades only. Export CSV any time. Sending grades to another system, such as Canvas or Google Classroom, is set up by the district.
Can I give one student extended time?Yes, per quiz: 1.25× to 4×, or no time limit. Only teachers and administrators see who has it.
Can I change a quiz after students start?The settings, yes. The questions lock so every attempt is scored the same way.
Do families see drafts?No. Students and families see feedback only after you share it.
Is my demo work saved?No. The demo runs in your browser and starts over when you reload or choose Reset demo.
Where do I get help?Your pilot lead first, then hello@educateos.io.

End-of-session checklist

  • I can open the pilot district's address and I land in Teacher Studio, or I've sent my sign-in ID to the administrator.
  • I can see my classes, or I've told the pilot lead they're missing.
  • I posted an assignment with a rubric and a due date in the demo.
  • I built a timed quiz and gave a student extended time in the demo.
  • I approved a grade and shared feedback, and found it in the gradebook.
  • I know whether my district's AI workspace is on, and that I don't enter student information in it.
  • I know what a "can't be sent" message means and what to do about it.
  • I have the printed quickstart, my pilot lead's contact and hello@educateos.io.
  • I know the date of the first check-in.

The hands-on tasks were done in the public demo's fictional Northfield district. The AI segment was checked against the product's code, because the public demo has no AI workspace.
