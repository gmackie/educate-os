Founding pilot · For teachers

Teacher quickstart

For teachers in an EducateOS founding pilot: one school in your district, a few teachers, about ten weeks. One task a page, so you can print the pages you need.

Practice in the demoKickoff training outline
The pilot's one rule about data. Until your district signs a data processing agreement with EducateOS, use only made-up (synthetic) students and staff accounts. Don't enter real student names, work or grades, and don't paste them into the AI workspace. Your pilot lead will tell you when that changes.

Practice any time at educateos.io/demo. It needs no sign-in, runs in your browser and uses a fictional district. Choose a teacher in the picker: Daniel Yazzie (grade 1), Fatima Al-Sayed (grade 4), Tomás Ortega (World History 7) or Sam Rivera (English 9). Reloading the page starts the demo over.

Task 1

Sign in

  1. Open your district's EducateOS address. Your pilot lead gives it to you. It is your district's own web address, such as yourdistrict.educateos.io or a district domain.
    Address:  
  2. Sign in the way your district set up, usually your school Google or Microsoft account, or a one-time code sent to your school email.
  3. As a teacher you land in Teacher Studio. The page says "Opening Teacher Studio" first.

If you see "You're signed in, but you haven't been added to this school yet", your sign-in worked but your account isn't linked to the school's records. Choose Copy your sign-in ID, send it to your pilot lead or school administrator, and reload the page once they've added you.

Sign out is in the account menu, next to your name.

Task 2

Your classes

My classes lists each class you teach, with how many students are enrolled. A co-teacher's classes show a Co-teacher badge. If you see "No classes yet", your district hasn't added you to a class: tell your pilot lead.

Open a class. Its tabs, in order: Roster, Modules, Resources, Create activity, Submissions, Gradebook, Announcements, Discussions, Quizzes.

  • Roster: each student, whether they're enrolled, and how many activities they've turned in. Your district's roster decides who is in a class; you can't add students yourself.
  • Modules: the units of the course. Add a course brings in a course from your district's course library as drafts you can edit. Teacher guides and answer keys arrive as teacher-only notes, which students never see.
Task 3

Post an assignment

In a class, open Create activity.

  1. Template: Writing task, Science investigation, Primary-source analysis, Problem set, or, for K-2, Picture response and Read and respond.
  2. About the activity: a Title and an Overview for students, then the template's own fields. A writing task has a Prompt, Kind of writing, Minimum length in words and Sources to cite.
  3. Optional: Learning goals (standards), Resources your class already has, and notes.
  4. AI help: None, Hints only or Full. Your district may allow less. If it does, you're told when you preview.
  5. Rubric: No rubric, a rubric from an earlier activity in this class, or a new one (Rubric title, Criteria one per line, Save and use this rubric).
  6. Choose Preview as student. You see the activity as a student will, and how it is assessed.
  7. Under Publish and assign, set a Due date (leave it empty for the end of the term) and choose Publish and assign. Back to editing returns to the form.

Once published, students in the class can open it. Later changes make a new version and don't change what students already have. Their work appears under Submissions.

Task 4

Build a quiz, set a time limit and give a student extended time

In a class, open Quizzes.

  1. Type a Quiz title and choose Create quiz.
  2. Under Settings: Attempts allowed, Time limit in minutes (optional), Opens and Closes (optional), whether each student sees the questions or choices in their own order, when students see the correct answers, and Which attempt counts.
  3. Add questions: Add multiple choice, Add multiple select, Add true or false, Add number, Add short answer or Add essay. Each has its points and its correct answer.
  4. Release scores as grades for: leave "A new gradebook assignment for this quiz, made when you publish it", or pick an existing assignment. Optionally Place it in a module.
  5. Choose Save quiz, then Publish. Unpublish makes it a draft again. Once students have started, the questions lock so every attempt is scored against the same key; settings can still change.

Extended time

At the bottom of the quiz page, under Extended time, choose the Student, then the Time: 1.25×, 1.5×, 2×, 3×, 4× or No time limit. With a 20-minute limit, 1.5× shows as "1.5× time (30 minutes)". Choose Give extended time. Each student with extended time is listed, with a button beside them to remove it.

  • Use the amount the student's IEP or 504 plan sets.
  • Only the class's teachers and district administrators see this list. Each student sees only their own time.
  • An attempt already in progress gets the extra time at once.
  • Extended time does nothing until the quiz has a time limit.

Afterwards, Results and grading shows each student's score and anything marked Needs grading (short answers and essays). Release scores sends them to the gradebook as official grades.

Task 5

Gradebook basics

In a class, open Gradebook.

  • It shows official grades only: a grade you approved, or quiz scores you released. A draft score or a suggestion never appears here.
  • Choose a cell (or move to it with the arrow keys and press Enter) to mark it Excused, Missing or Late. Marks change how totals add up; they never change a grade.
  • Gradebook settings: add all scores together or Weighted by group, combine by points or percent, count missing work as zero, assignment groups with a Weight (%) and Drop lowest, and the letter-grade scale.
  • Export CSV downloads the gradebook.

Grading written work

  1. Submissions, pick the activity, then Review [student]'s work.
  2. Choose a level for each rubric criterion and Save score. Add comments.
  3. Approve official grade. It asks once more before the grade becomes official.
  4. Share feedback with student is a separate choice. The student sees your comments and scores only after you share them.
Task 6

Your AI tools

The pilot starts with AI for teachers. Your district decides whether it is on. If it is, AI workspace appears in the menu and opens at /ai on your district's address.

What they do

  • The AI workspace's home, Teaching companion, has a Start a conversation box and four starters: Plan a lesson, Create practice questions, Adapt an activity and Draft a family update. Each asks you questions first, then drafts something for you to review.
  • On a student's work page, Ask AI about this opens the AI workspace about that submission. It appears only where your district has AI.
  • Teacher starter blueprints, In development: Week Ahead (reads one Google Calendar), Research Kit (web search results with their sources) and Assignment Companion (coursework from one Google Classroom course). They are read-only, keep your preparation notes, and make no AI calls. Use them only if your pilot lead says your district has added them.

What they don't do

  • They don't grade. Suggested scores count only when you save them as your own and approve the grade.
  • They don't send anything to students or families. A draft reaches them only if you put it into Teacher Studio and publish it. Anything an agent would write elsewhere, such as an email, waits for your approval.
  • They can be wrong. Check facts, dates and sources before you use a draft.
  • During the pilot, don't paste student information into a prompt.

Your district sets the policy

Your district chooses whether AI is on, which models, and how much each person may spend each month. A class can be stricter than the district, never looser. AI help on an activity is how you choose for students.

Requests are screened

Every AI request goes through the AI call guard in your district's own Cloudflare account before it reaches a model. Where your district's settings call for it, Hall Monitor, the screening service, checks the message first, and can check the reply before you see it.

If something is flagged, you get a short message in the conversation instead of a reply. It never says why and never quotes you. The default wording, written for students (your district can change it):

What happenedWhat you see
Your message was flagged"This message can't be sent to the AI assistant. Try rephrasing it, or ask your teacher for help."
The reply was flagged"The AI assistant's reply can't be shown. Try asking in a different way, or ask your teacher for help."
It reads as someone in distressA supportive message pointing to a counselor or another trusted adult.
Screening can't run right now"The AI assistant isn't available right now. You can keep working without it, or ask your teacher." Your district can let staff requests go ahead unscreened while screening is down.
The monthly AI budget is used up"The AI budget for this work has been used up. You can keep working without the assistant."
Your account isn't linked yet"Your account isn't linked to your school's records yet, so the AI assistant is off. Ask your school's administrator to link it."

Nothing else happens to you: no alert, no strike. Rephrase, or carry on without the AI. If a message was stopped that shouldn't have been, tell your pilot lead what you were trying to do; don't resend the text by email. The district keeps no per-message record of these checks; administrators see AI spend.

Task 7

Where your data goes

  • EducateOS runs in your district's own Cloudflare account. Classes, work and grades are stored there, and your district is their custodian.
  • AI requests go from that account to the model your district chose, through the AI call guard. By default, prompts are kept out of the AI gateway's logs.
  • EducateOS staff can't reach your district unless your district lets them, and everything they do is logged.
  • No advertising, and no sale of data.
  • During the pilot: synthetic or staff-only data until the DPA is signed.

More: Student privacy at EducateOS.
