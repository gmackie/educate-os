Student AI guidelines

Short, plain rules for using AI, written for four grade bands. Each band prints as one classroom poster. Teach them before students use any AI tool.

Grades K–2 Grades 3–5 Grades 6–8 Grades 9–12 Print the posters
For teachers. In the first phase of the founding pilot, students don't use EducateOS AI; these rules are for when the district turns it on, and for any other AI tool students meet. Why the rules say what they say: the bundled tutors give hints, not answers, but that is an instruction to the model, not a guarantee, so students still check. A message that looks like an email address, phone number or street address is never sent to a tutor's model, but other personal details aren't caught that way. A tutor keeps one conversation, which a learner can export for you, so a tutor you share with the class isn't private from you; the posters say "your teacher may see your chats". Every student message and reply is screened by Hall Monitor, and the AI never tells anyone what a student wrote, so the posters send students to a trusted adult. To print one band, choose its pages in the print dialog. For staff, see the staff AI use guidance. Editable copy: docs/educateos/pilot/student-ai-guidelines.md.
Grades K–2

Using AI helpers

  1. Ask a grown-up first. Use an AI helper only when your teacher says it's time.
  2. Keep secrets safe. Never tell the AI your full name, where you live, your phone number or your passwords.
  3. AI can make mistakes. If something sounds wrong or strange, ask your teacher.
  4. Your teacher may see your chats. Write only what you'd be happy for your teacher to read.
  5. Feeling worried or sad? Tell a grown-up. The AI can't help you the way a person can.
  6. Be kind. Type to the AI the way you'd talk to a classmate.
  7. Tell the truth. If the AI helped you, say so.
Grades 3–5

Using AI helpers

  1. Use AI when your teacher says you can, and only for what they said.
  2. AI gives hints, you do the thinking. The tutor helps you find the next step. The answer is yours to work out.
  3. Check it. AI can sound sure and still be wrong. Check facts in your book, a trusted website or with your teacher.
  4. Keep personal information private. Don't share your full name, address, phone number, birthday, passwords, or private things about you, your family or your friends.
  5. Your teacher may see your AI chats. Don't write anything you wouldn't want your teacher to read.
  6. Be honest. Tell your teacher when you used AI and what it helped with.
  7. If the AI won't answer, that's on purpose. Some questions are stopped for safety. If something is bothering you, talk to a trusted adult. The AI can't get help for you.
  8. Stuck or unsure? Ask your teacher.
Grades 6–8

Using AI for learning

  1. Follow your teacher's rules for each assignment. Some work allows AI, some doesn't. When in doubt, ask before you use it.
  2. Your work is your own. Use AI to brainstorm, get hints or check your understanding, not to produce work you hand in as yours.
  3. Say when you used AI. Note what you asked it and how it helped, the way you'd cite a source.
  4. Verify everything. AI can invent facts, quotes and sources. Check important claims against a reliable source before you use them.
  5. Protect personal information, yours and other people's: no full names, addresses, phone numbers, passwords, photos or private details.
  6. Your teacher may see your AI chats. Treat them like schoolwork, not a diary.
  7. Blocked messages are part of the safety system. If something worries you or a friend, tell a trusted adult. The AI won't tell anyone.
  8. Be respectful. Don't try to trick the AI into breaking school rules.
Grades 9–12

Using AI responsibly

  1. Know the rules for each assignment. Your teacher decides whether and how AI may be used. If it isn't stated, ask.
  2. Do your own thinking. Use AI to explore ideas, get feedback, practice or find where you're stuck. Don't submit AI-generated work as your own.
  3. Disclose and cite. Say which AI tool you used, what you asked and what you kept. Cite the original sources, not the AI.
  4. Verify. AI output can be wrong, out of date, biased or made up, including citations. You're responsible for what you submit.
  5. Protect privacy. Don't enter personal information about yourself or anyone else: names, contact details, IDs, passwords, health or family matters, photos.
  6. Assume your teacher can read your AI chats. Write accordingly.
  7. Understand the safety checks. Messages and replies are screened, and some are stopped. If you or someone you know needs help, talk to a trusted adult or counselor. AI is not a substitute.
  8. Use it fairly. Don't use AI to harass, impersonate or deceive anyone, or to get around school rules.
