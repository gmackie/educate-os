Accessibility conformance report

How the EducateOS district apps measure against WCAG 2.1 Level A and AA, laid out like a VPAT. It covers what we tested, how, what we fixed and what is still open. We've written it to be accurate, not flattering.

Report date November 13, 2026. The canonical copy, with the full method and every finding, is docs/educateos/accessibility.md in the open-source repository.

The short version

EducateOS partially conforms to WCAG 2.1 Level AA. Automated and programmatic checks of 143 screens found 14 failures, and all 14 are fixed. Of the 50 Level A and AA success criteria: 39 Supports, 3 Partially supports, 0 Does not support, 8 Not applicable.

No screen reader or other assistive technology has been used to test EducateOS yet. "Supports" below means our checks found no failure on the screens we audited. It is not a claim confirmed with assistive technology, and we will update this report when that testing is done.

Product and report

Product and report information
ProductEducateOS district apps: Learning Home (students and families), Teacher Studio, and the district admin console
VersionThe open-source main branch as of November 13, 2026, run as the zero-login demo at educateos.io/demo. That demo runs the same apps over the same district application as an installed district.
StandardWeb Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1, Level A and Level AA. This report doesn't cover Section 508 or EN 301 549 separately; both incorporate these WCAG 2.1 criteria for web content.
Evaluation methodsaxe-core 4.14.0 (WCAG 2.0/2.1 A and AA rules) driven by Playwright 1.64.1 in Chromium 156. The run covered 143 screens across 12 demo personas, in a light and a dark theme and at 320 CSS pixels wide. Programmatic checks covered keyboard focus order, names and visible focus; reflow; form-control contrast; text spacing; and status messages. Focus rings were inspected visually, and the code was reviewed for the criteria tools can't check. The audit script is in the repository and anyone can re-run it.
Not evaluatedScreen readers and other assistive technology. Also: browsers other than Chromium, the AI workspace, the Clever-rostered demo district's portal, sign-in, LTI launches from an LMS, and the mobile app.
Contacthello@educateos.io

Terms

  • Supports Our checks found no failure of the criterion on the screens audited.
  • Partially supports Some functionality does not meet the criterion.
  • Does not support Most functionality does not meet the criterion.
  • Not applicable The criterion is not relevant to the product.

1.1.1 Non-text Content (Level A)Supportsaxe's image, SVG and role=img rules pass. Decorative icons are hidden. Uploaded images and files carry their title as their name. The young learners' drawing step offers a photo upload. Teachers write the text alternatives for their own content.
1.2.1 Audio-only and Video-only (Prerecorded) (Level A)Not applicableThe apps contain no prerecorded media of their own. Media a teacher or student uploads plays in the browser's player, and its alternative is the author's to provide.
1.2.2 Captions (Prerecorded) (Level A)Not applicableAs 1.2.1.
1.2.3 Audio Description or Media Alternative (Prerecorded) (Level A)Not applicableAs 1.2.1.
1.3.1 Info and Relationships (Level A)Supportsaxe's structure, table, list and form-label rules pass. Every screen has landmarks and one h1. Tables have captions and headers, and radio groups are fieldsets with legends. Not yet confirmed with a screen reader.
1.3.2 Meaningful Sequence (Level A)SupportsDOM order matches the visual order: the keyboard walks follow reading order on every screen.
1.3.3 Sensory Characteristics (Level A)SupportsNo instruction relying on shape, size, position or sound alone was seen in the screens reviewed. They weren't reviewed exhaustively.
1.4.1 Use of Color (Level A)SupportsStatuses are words in badges, not colour alone. axe's link-in-text-block rule passes.
1.4.2 Audio Control (Level A)SupportsNothing plays automatically. "Read to me" starts on request and stops when pressed again.
2.1.1 Keyboard (Level A)SupportsEvery control on every screen is reachable by Tab. Wide tables scroll by keyboard. Freehand drawing needs a pointer, which is path-dependent input the criterion exempts, and the step offers a photo upload. The audit reaches controls by Tab but doesn't operate composite widgets (radio groups, comboboxes, the gradebook grid) by their arrow keys.
2.1.2 No Keyboard Trap (Level A)SupportsNo walk was trapped (see the full report).
2.1.4 Character Key Shortcuts (Level A)Not applicableThe apps have no single-character shortcuts. The demo's presenter keys are demo-only and work only with ?presenter.
2.2.1 Timing Adjustable (Level A)Partially supportsThe apps set no session time limits; sign-in sessions are the district's Cloudflare Access policy. A quiz's time limit is one its teacher sets for the assessment. A teacher of the class, or a district administrator, can give one student 1.25 to 4 times that limit, or no time limit, as an IEP or Section 504 plan sets it. The district's clock, on the server, enforces each student's own deadline, extends an attempt already in progress, and shows the student only their own time; API and UI tests check this (SCHO-383). A student can't extend or turn off a limit themselves, before or during an attempt, so a student whose plan isn't entered gets no adjustment. The quiz's close date still ends every attempt.
2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide (Level A)SupportsNo moving, blinking or auto-updating content other than loading indicators and a timed quiz's countdown, which is essential to the quiz.
2.3.1 Three Flashes or Below Threshold (Level A)SupportsNothing flashes.
2.4.1 Bypass Blocks (Level A)SupportsEach app's first control is "Skip to content", and every screen has landmarks.
2.4.2 Page Titled (Level A)SupportsEvery screen sets its own document title: its name, then its class or child where it has one, then the app, such as "Gradebook · English 9 · Period 3 · Teacher Studio" or "My grades · Learning Home". While a screen loads or can't load, its route's name stands in ("Activity · Learning Home"). The apps' tests check a unique title on each route. In the audit, no two of a persona's screens share a title, apart from its Home, which is the screen its app opens on (SCHO-382).
2.4.3 Focus Order (Level A)SupportsFocus follows the layout. Teacher Studio and the Learning Home move focus to the new page's heading when you navigate, the admin console moves it to the new screen's content, and dialogs keep focus inside. Not yet confirmed with a screen reader.
2.4.4 Link Purpose (In Context) (Level A)Supportsaxe's link-name rule passes. Icon-only links, such as Settings and Notifications, have names.
2.5.1 Pointer Gestures (Level A)SupportsNo multipoint or path-based gesture is needed to operate anything. Drawing is the exempt path-based case and has an upload alternative.
2.5.2 Pointer Cancellation (Level A)SupportsControls are native or Base UI buttons that act on release.
2.5.3 Label in Name (Level A)SupportsAfter the fixes, axe's label-content-name-mismatch passes everywhere.
2.5.4 Motion Actuation (Level A)Not applicableNothing responds to device motion.
3.1.1 Language of Page (Level A)Supportslang="en" on every app.
3.2.1 On Focus (Level A)SupportsNo walk caused navigation or a change of context.
3.2.2 On Input (Level A)SupportsNo change of context on input was seen: choosing an option doesn't navigate or submit.
3.3.1 Error Identification (Level A)SupportsCode review: failed actions and invalid forms state the error in text, in an alert region. Not every form's errors were triggered.
3.3.2 Labels or Instructions (Level A)Supportsaxe's label rules pass, and fields have visible labels.
4.1.1 Parsing (Level A)Supportsaxe's duplicate-id rules pass. The criterion is obsolete in WCAG 2.2.
4.1.2 Name, Role, Value (Level A)Supportsaxe's ARIA and name rules pass, and every focus stop has a name. Toggles expose pressed and expanded states. Not yet confirmed with a screen reader.

What we fixed

Everything the audit found that was fixable in the apps themselves:

  • Secondary, warning and placeholder text raised to at least 4.5:1 in both themes.
  • Checkbox, radio and switch boundaries raised to at least 3:1.
  • Three buttons whose accessible names didn't include their visible labels.
  • Wide tables that a keyboard couldn't scroll.
  • Six layouts that made the page scroll sideways on a 320-pixel screen.
  • A teacher can now give one student extended time, or no time limit, on a timed quiz, as IEP and 504 plans often require.

What is still open

  • Text field boundaries are 1.25 to 1.52:1 against the page, under the 3:1 that 1.4.11 asks for.
  • Links fade below 4.5:1 while the pointer is over them. This comes from the shared design system, so we're raising it upstream.
  • Testing with NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver and TalkBack hasn't been done.
  • A teacher or district administrator can extend a timed quiz for one student, or remove its limit, but a student can't extend a limit themselves. So 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable is rated Partially supports.

Each of the first three has an open issue in the project tracker, and this report changes in the same update as a fix.

Re-run it yourself

The audit is a script in the open-source repository. Build the demo, then run the audit:

pnpm exec vp run -F @educateos/demo build
pnpm --filter @educateos/demo a11y

This report describes the product as tested on the date above. It is not a legal opinion or a certification of compliance with the ADA, Section 508 or any other law, and each district should make its own assessment.
